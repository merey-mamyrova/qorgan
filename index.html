The problem

When it matters, a phone is too slow.

840 million women have experienced partner or sexual violence in their lifetime. World Health Organization, November 2025.

Asking for help takes steps

Unlock the phone, open an app, dial. Each step costs time you may not have.

Reaching for a phone can make things worse

In a tense moment, visible action can escalate the situation.

The people who can help are often nearby

Friends and family may be far away. Someone a few hundred meters off can arrive first.

How it works

Three moves, no screen needed.

Step 1

Hold

Press the wearable's button for two seconds. It vibrates once the app confirms the alert.

Step 2

Alert

The app shares your live location and starts recording audio. Your trusted contacts get a message in Telegram. They do not need the app.

Step 3

Nearby help

Qorgan users within 500 meters get a nearby-SOS alert. Partner venues, such as cafes and pharmacies with trained staff, are marked as places to go.

Everything Qorgan does

An app and a bracelet that back each other up.

SOS in two seconds

Hold the SOS button for two seconds. Your live location is shared, audio recording starts and your trusted contacts are alerted.

Alerts in Telegram

Your contacts get the alert in Telegram. They do not need the Qorgan app, they only connect their Telegram once. Tap “I’m OK” to end the alert.

Safety timer

Set a timer for a walk or a ride. If you do not confirm that you are safe in time, your contacts are alerted.

Fake call

A fake incoming call in the voice of your mom, dad, brother, sister or partner, recorded in the voice studio. Hold the Fake Call button on the home screen for an instant loud call, with audio recording started quietly.

Nearby SOS alert

Qorgan users within 500 meters are alerted when someone close to them needs help.

Safe map and Partners

A map of cafes, pharmacies and shops with trained staff. The nearest safe places also appear on the SOS screen, each with a route button. Partners are demo entries for now.

Safe Route

A walking route built over sidewalks and footpaths, with a safety score based on how close it passes to verified Partners, police and pharmacies.

SOS history

Every alert is saved with its time, locations and audio. The danger level comes from simple rules, not AI: low if you confirm you are safe within 2 minutes, medium for 2 to 6 minutes, high after 6 minutes or 3 missed check-ins.

Low-battery alert

If your phone is about to run out of battery, your trusted contact is told, so a silent phone is never a surprise.

Three languages

The whole interface works in English, Russian and Kazakh.

One button, two seconds

Hold the button for two seconds and the SOS starts on your phone. No screen, no unlocking.

Vibration confirmation

The bracelet vibrates once the app confirms that the alert was sent, so you know it worked without looking.

Pulse sensor

A MAX30102 sensor reads your heart rate and sends it to the app about every two seconds. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose anything.

Bluetooth link to your phone

The bracelet talks to your phone over Bluetooth Low Energy. GPS, microphone and internet stay in the phone, which keeps the bracelet small. Keep the phone with you, in a pocket or a bag.

Rechargeable

A small 250 mAh lithium battery, charged over USB-C.

Strap and enclosure

A 3D-printed enclosure with an adjustable strap. A smaller ring is the next form factor.

Our story

Why we built Qorgan, and how it grew.

  1. The idea

    Asking for help should not be this hard

    Qorgan started from a personal experience: we realised how hard it is to ask for help quietly and quickly when you feel threatened. Unlocking a phone, opening an app and dialing takes time, and everyone around you can see it.

  2. First prototype

    A clickable mock-up in five hours

    Our first version was a low-fidelity prototype built in about five hours. Most of its buttons did nothing yet, but it let us test the idea of one big SOS button with a safe map around it.

  3. The real app

    Flutter, real location, real maps

    Merey turned the mock-up into a working Flutter app: real geolocation, OpenStreetMap maps, audio recording, Telegram alerts, the safety timer, the fake call, translations into three languages and a Bluetooth link to the bracelet.

  4. First bracelet

    Button, motor and Bluetooth on a breadboard

    Tomiris built the first bracelet from an ESP32 board, a button and a vibration motor, then added a pulse sensor. It first lived on a breadboard: hold the button for two seconds, the phone starts the SOS, and the bracelet vibrates in reply.

  5. Feedback

    A school jury asked us to go smaller

    When we defended Qorgan at school, the jury’s advice was clear: make the device smaller and more discreet. That is why a ring is where we are heading.

  6. October 2026

    A finished bracelet, and the next stage

    The bracelet is now a finished 3D-printed piece with all the electronics inside, and it works. Qorgan also advanced to the network stage of Kazakhstan’s Republican Scientific Projects Competition.

Questions and answers

What people ask us.

Do my contacts need to install the app?

No. They get the alert in Telegram. They connect their Telegram once so the alert can reach them, and that is all.

Does the bracelet work without my phone?

No. The bracelet has no GPS or microphone of its own. It sends your button press to your phone over Bluetooth, and the phone shares the location, records audio and sends the alert. Keep the phone with you, in a pocket or a bag.

Can I trigger an SOS by accident?

SOS needs a two-second hold, so a quick tap does nothing. If you do start one by mistake, tap “I’m OK” to end the alert.

Does Qorgan call the police or an ambulance?

No. Qorgan alerts the people you trust and shows safe places nearby. It does not replace emergency services, so call your local emergency number as well when you can.

Is Qorgan only for women?

No. The project began with the experience of girls, but Qorgan is built for anyone who feels unsafe: harassment, stalking, robbery, a physical threat, an accident or a medical emergency.

Who can see my location?

When you trigger an SOS, your trusted contacts get your location, and Qorgan users nearby can be alerted. Because misuse of a nearby alert is a real risk, we are designing it to be opt-in, with clear warnings about the risks of turning it on or off. A public privacy policy will come with the app release.

What does the pulse sensor do?

It reads your heart rate and streams it to the app about every two seconds. Qorgan is not a medical device and does not diagnose anything.

How is the danger level calculated?

From time and missed check-ins, with no AI. Low if you confirm you are safe within 2 minutes, medium between 2 and 6 minutes, high after 6 minutes or after 3 missed check-ins.

What are Qorgan Partners?

Cafes, pharmacies and shops with trained staff that you can walk into when you feel unsafe. In the app they are demo entries today. Real partner pilots are planned.

Why a bracelet, and not just an app?

A phone takes several steps: unlock, open, tap. The bracelet needs one two-second press, and nobody around you sees a screen. We started with a bracelet because it is easier to build, and a smaller ring is where we are heading.

Can I try Qorgan now?

Not publicly yet. The app and the bracelet work, and we are preparing the public release. Join the waitlist and we will write to you when testing opens.
